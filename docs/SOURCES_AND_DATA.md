# NASA Space Apps 2026 — data lineage and future mission milestones

Reviewed 2026-11-14. This document is intended to make the actual use of NASA source data easy for judges to verify. The game is an independent, educational prototype, not an endorsed mission-design tool.

## Challenge-resource NASA data used as input, not merely referenced

**NASA CubeSat 101**, NASA CubeSat Launch Initiative (2017), Section 1.1:
<https://www3.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/atoms/files/nasa_csli_cubesat_101_508.pdf>

- Source data: 1U 10 × 10 × 11 cm and approximately 1–1.33 kg; 3U 10 × 10 × 34 cm.
- Implemented in **src/lib/campaignMissions.ts** as `NASA_CUBESAT_REFERENCE` and in the flight review for special **NASA CubeSat 101 · 3U Rideshare** contracts. The player selects a 1U, 3U or no rideshare/dispenser option.
- Changing the selected format recalculates spacecraft launch mass, electrical draw and procurement cost; a 3U-only contract cannot pass readiness with an incompatible dispenser.
- **Fidelity**: 3U reference payload mass is calculated as 3 × 1.33 kg for game purposes; not asserted as a real 3U mass standard. Dispenser mass, cost and electrical demand are game assumptions. The historical 2017 PDF does not supersede an actual launch provider's current interface-control document.

The **Space Mission Design Game** challenge lists CubeSat 101 in its NASA Resources section. The judging authority decides eligibility; we do not claim to certify compliance.

## Other active NASA/JPL numerical sources

- JPL *Approximate Positions of the Planets* (1800–2050): <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html>. Numerical orbital elements and secular rates are stored in `src/lib/orbitalMechanics.ts` and used in heliocentric position calculations and planetary phase indicators.
- JPL *Astrodynamic Parameters*: <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html>. Gravitational parameters in orbital energy, circular-orbit speeds and Keplerian motion.
- NASA/NSSDC *Planetary Fact Sheet*: <https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/>. Planetary reference dimensions and environment.
- NASA GMAT (official challenge resource): <https://software.nasa.gov/software/GSC-17177-1>. Research reference only — the game does not execute GMAT. Optional higher-fidelity impulsive transfers are handled by **Orekit**, not GMAT.

## Planned, real-life NASA missions used for fictional support contracts

- **Europa Clipper**, December 2026 Earth gravity-assist flyby and April 2030 Jupiter orbit insertion: <https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/mission-timeline/>.
- **NEO Surveyor**, not-earlier-than September 2027 launch, asteroid/comet infrared telescope: <https://science.nasa.gov/mission/neo-surveyor/>.
- **SkyFall**, late-2028 planned Mars rotorcraft mission: <https://science.nasa.gov/mission/skyfall/>.
- **Dragonfly**, NET July 2028 launch, late-2034 Titan arrival: <https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dragonfly/>. Listed as a future mission in the references tab, not as a playable fake Saturn trajectory.

Mission titles, prizes, proposal/launch windows, energy thresholds, CubeSat dispenser costs, required payload masses, and the player's spacecraft are **fictional**. If a fictional NASA-inspired proposal period is missed, a **25% lower-paying follow-on contract** remains playable, under the original contract ID (only one reward). Original NASA milestones are not repeated, and main story unlocks do not depend on accepting them. In particular: the game asteroid is synthetic, its NEO Surveyor contract does not use the actual Sun-Earth L1 orbit; the Jupiter scenario is a flyby rather than Europa Clipper's Jupiter orbit insertion; SkyFall's actual reactor-powered transfer is not simulated. Spacecraft and real mission properties must not be conflated with gameplay parameters.

## v1.9 Galaxia-inspired orbital businesses, reusable spacecraft saves and launch-date physics

**Real source inspiration, not real prices or actual constellation hardware:**
- Galaxia **Onyx Edge Core + Blades / Virtual Satellite-as-a-Service (VSaS)**: https://gx.space/news-press-releases/galaxia-introduces-onyx-edge. A modular space computer and service concept; the game's mass 75 kg, peak draw 550 W, heat 495 W, procurement $28M, hosted compute revenue and data capacity are ALL fictional.
- Galaxia **OSPREY / Halifax Class**: https://gx.space/news-press-releases/galaxia-unveils-osprey-advancing-canadian-built-space-technology-for-defence-applications. Real Canadian company names are credited; the game's 60 kg, 410 W, 350 W waste heat, $24M hybrid RF/optical relay product and commercial customer subscription market are invented, not manufacturer figures.
- Canadian **Kepler Communications**: https://www.kepler.space/. The game does not claim Kepler supplied hardware, has launched the user's simulated constellation or offers the described network services.
- **No 14-satellite system is claimed to equal global Starlink coverage**; network footprint, orbital planes, spectrum licensing, actual thermal environment, cloud cybersecurity and RF/optical links are not dynamically simulated.

**Coupled economics and physics:** `src/lib/orbitalServiceModules.ts` owns modeled service modules; `src/lib/subsystemDesign.ts` adds module mass/active electrical power to batteries, generation, buses and radiator heat; `src/lib/missionEconomics.ts` adds procurement price. The `Infrastructure` research branch in `src/lib/technologyTree.ts` requires both cash and RP before either module is eligible for a real game deployment. `src/lib/orbitalNetworks.ts` validates installed module family, power and reserve, structure fit, radiator capacity, independent intact-satellite launcher packing, ground infrastructure and entire multi-launch invoice. Each spacecraft launches whole—no physically impossible splitting. `src/OrbitalInfrastructure.tsx` displays stages and starts paid timed deliveries. `src/lib/programEconomy.ts` adds recurring fictional revenue **only after commissioning**, with proportional ground/network operations outlay to the daily cash runway. Day skips and the live clock use the same economics.

**Reusable independent spacecraft designs:** `src/lib/spacecraftBlueprints.ts` stores validated immutable snapshots and mission-use lists, supports new, load, duplicate, rename, delete with old save migration; `src/SpacecraftLibrary.tsx` manages the user workflow. Actual flown designs automatically create a saved configuration on mission procurement. The tool does not claim full CAD interchange or a validated flight-qualification manifest.

**Launch date optimization:** `orekit-service/src/main/java/dev/spacerace/LaunchWindowMath.java` accepts up to 80 dated departure trials, each with departure Earth and arrival Mars/Jupiter position+velocity vectors from **JPL's approximate elements (1800–2050)**, and solves each via Orekit Lambert 2-body + Kepler propagation. The result is departure burn from a modeled Earth circular parking orbit; Mars orbit insertion is a simplified impulsive circularization at 300 km, Jupiter is explicitly a **flyby with no capture**. `src/lib/launchWindowForecast.ts` submits 32 sample dates (28-day steps), and `src/LaunchWindowForecast.tsx` compares future required Δv (including explicit game 5% margin) with actual installed spacecraft Δv and elapsed-day cash opportunity cost. Fixed mission flight duration, sampled dates and approximate ephemerides exclude low-thrust cruise, plane changes, launch ascent, finite burns, gravity assists, plane targeting, actual surface arrival and any globally guaranteed minimum. If Java is offline, the plotted Hohmann phase-penalty curve is a GAME heuristic, not a valid trajectory solution; the UI labels it accordingly. Earth/lunar missions never claim fictitious date-based launch energy minima.

## v1.8 official NASA and Canadian Space Agency GitHub and Flight Directorate model provenance

**NASA code inspected:** [NASA mission-viz](https://github.com/nasa/mission-viz) provides public web orrery and elliptical-orbit educational visualization examples. [NASA/GMAT](https://github.com/NASA/GMAT) is NASA's extensive open-source mission analysis/optimization software (far too large for a direct React game dependency). [NASA JEOD](https://github.com/nasa/jeod) integrates dynamic environments/vehicle states under NASA's Trick simulation platform, also NOT part of our game runtime. These are software *references*, not live data APIs. The game actually uses its own simplified Keplerian geometry + optional **Orekit 13.1.9** server.

**CSA official GitHub and data provenance:** 
- [NEOSSAT tutorial](https://github.com/asc-csa/NEOSSAT_Tutorial): public code teaching downloading/filtering/visualizing astronomical FITS CCD images from the Canadian Astronomy Data Centre and CSA, including NEO preprocessing. Game scenario: **Canadian Optical Asteroid Survey**, requiring an explicitly fictional NEOSSAT-inspired optical telescope. No NEOSSAT actual sensor mass, throughput, orbital elements or FITS dataset is assumed/embedded.
- [CASSIOPE tutorial](https://github.com/asc-csa/CASSIOPE-Tutorial): Fast Auroral Imager (FAI)/e-POP auroral observations, visualization and map projections. Some University of Calgary data access requires permission. Game scenario: **Canadian Auroral Research Satellite**, requiring a physically accounted fictional auroral sensor. This is not the actual e-POP/FAI spacecraft specification.
- [RADARSAT/EODMS tutorial](https://github.com/asc-csa/EO-Visualization-and-Search-Tutorial) and [RADARSAT-1 open-data scripts](https://github.com/asc-csa/radarsat1-scripts): teach querying Canadian EODMS and working with RADARSAT SAR raster imagery, some requiring accounts. Game scenario: **Canadian Arctic Radar Mapping** requires a modeled radar sensor with high power, heat, wet spacecraft mass, launch and procurement costs. Neither actual RADARSAT radar data nor any restricted imagery is embedded.
- All **actual source properties** are the mission/instrument/data family and external research/archive links. **Invented game properties** include platform/instrument mass, power, telemetry bit rate, budget, scientific payout, orbit and contractual timing. The scenario is not a procurement solicitation or actual CSA satellite.

**Flight Directorate numeric methods:** `src/lib/flightDirectoratePhysics.ts` uses JPL's [planetary approximate element table](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html) through `src/lib/orbitalMechanics.ts`, [Earth μ and radius](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html), Kepler's equation and vis-viva. It produces dimensioned Cartesian vectors over real physical time, not visual Bezier control points. Earth satellites use Earth-centered Keplerian elements including the actual selected perigee and eccentricity, and the moving craft is evaluated from elapsed mission time. The lunar fallback is a **single Earth-centered two-body 400-km-perigee to 384,400-km-apogee transfer** using Keplerian time of flight, oriented toward the Moon's approximate circular sidereal phase at first apogee (~5 days). It is NOT a solved Earth–Moon Lambert, lunar insertion, n-body propagation, descent, gravity assist or a certification-grade lunar mission. The Moon phase is an idealized 27.321661-day mean lunar orbit, with no ephemeris kernels.

For interplanetary flights, when `POST /api/lambert` returns a matching Orekit trajectory, its propagated 3D Sun-centered Cartesian path is used (plotted in 2D ecliptic XY). If unavailable, the fallback calculates a Kepler Hohmann ellipse from the JPL-derived Earth departure radius to the target's mean orbit, and propagates using Kepler's equation. The target planet marker moves with the game timestamp using the same JPL approximate planetary elements; the difference from the hypothetical Hohmann craft shows *miss distance*, NOT a claimed intercept. **Do not infer low-thrust Δv, gravity assists, actual planetary encounter or capture from these approximations.** The synthetic survey asteroid uses a model circular 1.35 AU marker, never an actual NeoWs orbit.

Optional backend: `orekit-service/src/main/java/dev/spacerace/EarthOrbitMath.java` provides Earth-centered (and half-orbit translunar injection) Keplerian samples through `POST /api/earth-orbit`, using Orekit `KeplerianOrbit` and `KeplerianPropagator`, EME2000 and Earth μ in SI units. This is a scientifically real two-body propagation method but omits J2/drag, Earth–Moon gravitational perturbations, Moon insertion, C3 launch injection, finite burns, spacecraft gravity gradient and actual transit navigation. The fallback is also physically propagated but only at the same simplified two-body fidelity. All rendering is an accelerated visualization of those calculations.

## v1.7 real NASA API, dated observation data and live mission conditions

The optional **NASA Mission Intelligence** screen uses NASA Open APIs: <https://api.nasa.gov/>. On opening the tab (or manually refreshing), it requests:
- `GET /DONKI/FLR`, `GET /DONKI/GST`, `GET /DONKI/SEP` for the last seven real UTC days; NASA CCMC DONKI reference <https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/>.
- `GET /neo/rest/v1/feed` for today's through seven days later: NASA JPL Near Earth Object Web Service, source catalog <https://api.nasa.gov/>.

DONKI is a NASA research/near-real-time event database, NOT an official future space-weather forecast, and NASA explicitly identifies <https://www.spaceweather.gov/> (NOAA SWPC) as the US government's official forecast source. Historical DONKI observations may inform research but not operational safety certifications. Solar flare X/M class and storm Kp event thresholds are mapped to **invented game contingency hours** (elevated +1 hour, high +2 hours). The modifier only applies to Earth/lunar mission battery backup if game and real UTC dates match within three days, and reported events are not older than 72 hours. The model does not simulate radiation exposure or guarantee spacecraft survivability. A missing API does not make missions fail.

NASA-reported NeoWs asteroid names, approximate size, Earth close-approach dates and miss distances can produce a new **fictional Earth-orbit optical observation mission**. The contract's **fictional payout scales with reported miss distance and size**, while **fictional required science-mass and electrical demand scale with reported size and approach velocity**. The orbital ellipse, game deadline and launch procurement remain invented, and the mission is **not a physical rendezvous with the named asteroid**; the separate synthetic 1.35 AU asteroid story target remains synthetic. On success, the mission uses existing launch billing and awards standard contract research points.

To conserve NASA quota, the NASA snapshot persists in `localStorage` for four hours; the client uses public `DEMO_KEY` (30 hourly requests per IP, 50 daily) when deployed without a proxy. On a local Vite server, `vite.config.ts` proxies `/nasa-api` and injects the optional `NASA_API_KEY` from a gitignored `.env.local`. **Never use `VITE_NASA_API_KEY`: that would expose the personal key in distributed browser source code.** If no data can be retrieved, mission data falls back to cached readings or neutral physical assumptions. Since there is no server included for a production static hosting deployment, custom keys are NOT usable privately in the front-end hosted build without adding a real backend/proxy.

Key implementation: `src/lib/nasaMissionClient.ts` (network/cache/errors), `src/lib/nasaMissionIntelligence.ts` (sanitized parsing, UTC overlap criteria and fictional asteroid offer), `src/NASAMissionIntelligence.tsx` (briefings, source links, actions), `src/lib/campaignMissions.ts` (real-time contingency requirement).

This does **not** state that a reused Falcon 9 has a 22-tonne payload or that any specific LEO mission is $74M. Game assumptions use a conservative **15,500 kg** reusable LEO scenario with a $74M reference price for comparison, not a real quote.
- **Starship published capability:** <https://new.spacex.com/vehicles/starship> advertises the 100+ tonne *design* goal for fully reusable LEO operations; the game's **100,000 kg** capacity and **$180M** launch fee are purely speculative comparison figures, not actual commercially offered pricing. Falcon Heavy game payload and price, additional fleet-level reuse discounts, orbital assembly economics, and integration weight penalties are equally fictional.
- **Game rocket math:** `src/lib/engineering.ts` calculates ideal ascent Δv and liftoff thrust-to-weight for the user's custom rocket. `src/lib/launchLogistics.ts` computes an indicative LEO payload limit for that rocket at 9,300 m/s reference ascent demand and minimum 1.15 TWR, then uses it, or a separately identified service capacity, in an itemized multi-flight manifest. **The full spacecraft and kick-stage WET mass, including propellant, must be launched;** additional dock/join kits add to the manifest. Each flight is paid separately. No real multi-body ascent trajectory, recovery dynamics or mechanical docking simulation is claimed.
- **Lunar logistics:** `src/lib/launchLogistics.ts` lists each required payload component and masses in three fictional Moon Base stages. A chemical lander dry-mass estimate uses a fixed structure allowance and 14% of payload; a 5,550 m/s assumed combined TLI/LOI/descent-equivalent ideal Δv at 345s assumed Isp computes propellant mass via the rocket equation. Each cargo mission includes launcher invoices, itemized hardware procurement and optional docking fees. `src/MoonBase.tsx` shows the manifest and a timed lunar transfer. Only cargo receipt plus separate construction investment activates revenue; neither a genuine Moon landing guidance solution nor verified NASA vehicle integration is supplied.
- **Persistence:** `src/App.tsx` stores rocket fleet generation, provider, reuse flight counts, orbital docking license, each assembly-stage inventory and separately timed Moon freight delivery in the original campaign save, migrating older purchased Moon Base levels as already having received supplies.

## Propellant depletion, power-constrained thrust and 3D architecture (v1.3)

- The fundamental governing relation **ṁ = F/(Isp · g₀)** and Tsiolkovsky **Δv = Isp · g₀ · ln(m₀/mf)** are implemented in `src/lib/spacecraftSystems.ts` and `src/lib/propulsionBurn.ts`; no fuel is instantaneously deleted to obtain mission Δv. Propellant mass drains throughout the burn and its remaining mass stays aboard.
- Distinguish **ideal all-propellant Δv** (independent of thrust count at fixed wet mass) from **deliverable mission-window Δv** (finite rated engine thrust, time available, firing duty, and whole powered engine count). The distinction explains why reducing engine count can increase total potential Δv while lowering delivered Δv during a short opportunity. Engine counts change dry mass through engine and PPU mass.
- Dual propulsion families share a single time budget and burn sequentially in the selected order. Power left after housekeeping determines how many rated engines can fire; no unpublished throttle efficiency curve is invented.
- `src/lib/structureSizing.ts` computes unique rear nozzle mounts and minimum frame width for the installed engine count. Increasing visual bus frame scale also adds dry mass and an illustrative manufacturing cost; no qualification or structural stress analysis is performed.
- The physics is preliminary: **no numerical finite-thrust orbit propagation, gravity losses during long burns, varying power across a trajectory, eclipse duty integration, flow regulation, propellant boiloff, gimbals, thruster plume interaction or engine qualification**.

## Time and transfer fidelity

- Open browser tab advances **one game day every two real minutes**. Backgrounded/closed tabs do not accumulate real time. Newly started games map the user's browser calendar date to the 2026-11-14 game epoch; previously saved game days remain intact.
- Calendar event cards are pinned to real NASA-reported month/year milestones, but the game deadlines around those milestones are **deliberately invented** for gameplay.
- Browser orbital calculations are two-body Earth orbit / circular-orbit Hohmann educational approximations using JPL elements for rough planetary phase comparison. Orekit can optionally return an impulsive Lambert solution for Mars and Jupiter. Finite-thrust routing, launch-vehicle certification, actual operational flight windows, radiation qualification and flight hardware designs are not modeled.
